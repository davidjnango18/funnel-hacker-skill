# Direct Response: Accountable Persuasion

## Scope

Operational synthesis of Hopkins (`SRC-001`), Schwartz (`SRC-002`), CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`), and MECLABS (`SRC-012`). Use for ads, landers, VSLs, emails, offers, and measurable next actions. This note does not supply performance benchmarks.

## Shared principles

1. **Start with an existing customer state.** Desire, awareness, traffic source, and current motivation constrain what a message can accomplish.
2. **Give each asset one observable job.** Attention, understanding, belief, opt-in, purchase, or another defined action.
3. **Make a specific, credible argument.** Detail should reduce uncertainty, not inflate claims.
4. **Preserve continuity.** The destination must fulfill the expectation created by the source message.
5. **Test rather than canonize.** A competitor pattern or classic maxim is a hypothesis until owned evidence supports it.
6. **Judge the business outcome.** A higher click or conversion rate can coexist with weaker qualification, margin, retention, or brand effects.

## Operational model

For each funnel asset record:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Audience state | What does the prospect already know, want, and believe? |
| Asset job | Which micro-decision should this asset earn? |
| Proposition | Why take this action rather than an alternative or no action? |
| Proof | What evidence supports the material claims? |
| Friction/anxiety | What effort or concern blocks progress? |
| Response | What observable action follows? |
| Guardrail | What downstream metric could worsen? |

## Diagnostic workflow

1. Classify the evidence and assign source IDs.
2. Infer an awareness state only from traffic context, language, behavior, or research—and label the inference.
3. State the asset's intended decision.
4. Audit proposition, proof, friction, anxiety, and next-step continuity.
5. Identify the smallest falsifiable change.
6. Test with owned instrumentation; do not import competitor or historical response rates.

## Tensions to preserve

- **Hopkins vs. Sharp (`SRC-004`):** immediate response is highly useful but does not capture all advertising effects. Maintain direct-response metrics and brand/availability measures.
- **Schwartz vs. “create demand” language:** messaging can educate or crystallize a need, but the claim that copy created demand is stronger than observable evidence normally permits.
- **Long copy vs. low friction:** more information can build belief while adding effort. Match depth to risk, complexity, awareness, and observed questions.
- **Heuristics vs. experiments:** diagnostic scores organize thinking; they do not estimate lift.
