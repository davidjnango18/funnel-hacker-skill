# Awareness and Market Sophistication

## Scope

Primary model: Schwartz (`SRC-002`). Supporting lenses: Hopkins (`SRC-001`), Dunford (`SRC-005`), Hormozi (`SRC-008`, `SRC-009`), and CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`). Use for message entry, hook/angle selection, proof depth, and funnel sequencing.

## Two separate diagnoses

- **Awareness:** what this audience knows about the problem, solutions, product, and offer now.
- **Sophistication:** how much similar claim and mechanism language the market has already encountered.

Do not collapse them. A buyer can be solution-aware in a highly sophisticated category, or product-aware in a relatively new one.

## Awareness routing

| State | Lead with | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most aware | concrete offer, terms, availability, reason to act | re-educating at length without need |
| Product aware | differentiated value, proof, objection resolution | generic category education |
| Solution aware | desired outcome, category comparison, credible bridge to product | unexplained brand-first claims |
| Problem aware | problem language, stakes, mechanism of solution | premature price/CTA pressure |
| Unaware | situation, identity, observation, or useful story that reveals relevance | direct product claim with no context |

## Sophistication routing

1. **New claim:** direct promise may be sufficient.
2. **Repeated claim:** specificity or stronger proof may restore credibility.
3. **Crowded claim set:** a genuine explanatory mechanism can differentiate.
4. **Crowded mechanisms:** improve, narrow, or substantiate the mechanism.
5. **Exhausted category language:** enter through identity, experience, or reframing, then rebuild the argument.

## Evidence needed before classification

- current competitor ads and destinations;
- search/query language and channel intent;
- customer interviews, surveys, sales/support records;
- observed objections and alternative solutions;
- repetition of claims/mechanisms across the category;
- the visitor's entry source and prior exposure.

Demographics alone are insufficient. Store the result as a hypothesis when direct research is missing.

## Applications by asset

- **Hooks/ads:** create variants for materially different entry states, not cosmetic rewrites.
- **Landing pages:** continue the ad's starting point and advance to the next required belief.
- **VSL/webinar:** sequence from current belief through mechanism, proof, offer, and fit.
- **Lead magnets:** solve a narrow problem appropriate to awareness and connect to a legitimate next job.
- **Email:** use behavior and prior consumption to avoid repeating or skipping necessary context.

## Cross-source constraints

- Dunford's category frame can accelerate comprehension for solution-aware buyers, but can confuse unaware buyers if introduced without a problem bridge.
- Hormozi's value stack cannot compensate for a message that assumes the wrong awareness state.
- Hopkins's specificity and CXL's voice-of-customer research help validate and express each state.
- A novel mechanism must be truthful and explanatory; novelty is not evidence.
