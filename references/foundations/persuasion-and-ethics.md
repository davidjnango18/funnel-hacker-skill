# Persuasion, Proof, and Ethical Boundaries

## Scope

Primary source: Cialdini (`SRC-003`). Applications are cross-checked with Walker (`SRC-007`), Hormozi (`SRC-008`), MECLABS (`SRC-012`), and CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`).

## Operating principle

Persuasion cues should lower legitimate decision cost by making relevant reality easier to evaluate. They must not fabricate evidence, restrict choice deceptively, or convert uncertainty into pressure.

## Evidence-first cue record

For each observed or proposed cue capture:

| Field | Example question |
| --- | --- |
| Principle | Reciprocity, commitment, social proof, liking, authority, or scarcity? |
| Observable implementation | What does the page/message actually show? |
| Underlying fact | What makes the cue true? |
| Decision uncertainty | Which specific concern should it resolve? |
| Placement | Is it close to the claim or action? |
| Limitation | Is the evidence typical, current, relevant, and qualified? |
| Risk | Could it coerce, mislead, or obscure material terms? |

## Ethical applications

- **Reciprocity:** give useful value with no hidden obligation.
- **Commitment:** let users express preferences or progress voluntarily; do not treat a micro-action as broad consent.
- **Social proof:** use verified, relevant, and appropriately qualified evidence.
- **Liking:** build a recognizable, respectful voice without fake intimacy.
- **Authority:** show expertise relevant to the claim and disclose material relationships.
- **Scarcity:** state a real constraint, its basis, and its actual end condition.

## Red flags

- reset countdowns or recurring “last chance” messages;
- stock/capacity claims with no operational basis;
- testimonials without identity/permission or with atypical outcomes presented as normal;
- irrelevant seals, credentials, media logos, or “as seen in” claims;
- preselected commitments, obstructed cancellation, or consent bundling;
- shame, fear, or identity pressure disproportionate to the decision;
- bonuses that conceal the core economics or material conditions.

## Cross-source findings

- Walker's mental triggers overlap with Cialdini, but the launch packaging is practitioner guidance; do not upgrade it to research-backed merely because a principle name matches.
- MECLABS helps locate proof near anxiety, while CXL requires evidence that the anxiety actually exists.
- Hormozi's scarcity, urgency, and guarantees can improve clarity/risk allocation when true; when simulated, they become abuse signals.
- Conversion impact never excuses false claims or dark patterns.

## Validation rule

No persuasion principle is a measured cause in a specific funnel until a valid owned analysis supports that conclusion. Report it as an observed cue and, when appropriate, a testable hypothesis.
