# Positioning, Distinctiveness, and Brand Growth

## Scope

Synthesis of Dunford (`SRC-005`) and Sharp (`SRC-004`), with Schwartz (`SRC-002`) and CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`) as research/application bridges.

## The productive tension

Dunford emphasizes the customer context in which a product is clearly the best fit. Sharp emphasizes broad category reach, penetration, distinctive assets, and ease of buying. These are not automatically contradictory:

- use **positioning** to make value understandable relative to real alternatives;
- use **distinctive assets** so the brand is recognized across encounters;
- use **broad enough reach and availability** to acquire category buyers beyond a tiny core;
- test how narrowly message and media should be focused for the business stage and category.

## Integrated model

1. Identify what buyers would do without the product.
2. Identify genuinely unique attributes relative to those alternatives.
3. Map attributes to provable value.
4. Define conditions that make that value especially relevant.
5. Choose a category frame that creates helpful expectations.
6. Encode the brand with consistent distinctive assets.
7. Reach plausible category buyers across buying situations.
8. Make the offer easy to notice, access, and buy.
9. Monitor penetration, recognition, response, fit, and downstream value.

## Diagnostic matrix

| Symptom | Positioning question | Brand-growth question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prospects do not understand the product | Is the category/comparison frame wrong? | Are recognizable category and brand cues missing? |
| High response from a tiny segment | Is this the best-fit beachhead? | Is targeting preventing scalable reach? |
| Creative looks generic | Is differentiated value absent? | Are distinctive assets weak or inconsistent? |
| Strong traffic, weak sales | Is the value/proof relevant? | Is the product/payment/delivery actually available? |
| High repeat metrics | Is fit genuinely exceptional? | Could brand size and selection explain the pattern? |

## What to test empirically

- narrow best-fit targeting versus broader category reach;
- category label and comparison frame;
- distinctive-asset consistency and brand attribution;
- value messages for different buying situations;
- availability barriers by market, device, channel, and payment method.

Do not declare a winner from short-term conversion alone. Include acquisition quality, penetration, recognition, margin, retention, and practical availability where measurable.
