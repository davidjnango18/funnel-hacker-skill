# Lead Generation and Lead Magnets

## Scope

Primary framework: Hormozi Leads (`SRC-009`). Supporting principles: Hopkins (`SRC-001`), Schwartz (`SRC-002`), Sharp (`SRC-004`), and CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`).

## Definitions

- **Contact:** a record or identifier.
- **Engaged lead:** a person who performs a defined interest action.
- **Qualified lead:** an engaged lead meeting explicit fit/intent criteria.
- **Customer:** a completed purchase or contract under the business's definition.

Never report these as interchangeable counts.

## Channel map

- warm one-to-one outreach;
- free/public content;
- cold one-to-one outreach;
- paid one-to-many advertising;
- amplification/execution through customers, employees, agencies, or partners.

For each channel record consent basis, audience, offer, engagement event, cost, capacity, qualification, and downstream outcome.

## Lead-magnet design

1. Select a narrow, meaningful problem.
2. Ensure solving it creates real value and a coherent next job.
3. Choose diagnosis/reveal, sample/trial, or one step of a larger process.
4. Deliver through a tool, information, service, or physical asset appropriate to the job.
5. Reduce consumption effort.
6. Make the next action explicit and proportionate.
7. Measure consumption, qualification, activation, purchase, cost, and complaints—not opt-ins alone.

## Diagnostic signals

- high opt-ins but low consumption or qualification;
- a giveaway attracts people who do not face the paid problem;
- “free” conceals shipping, trial renewal, data use, or sales contact;
- a diagnostic exaggerates risk to manufacture the next problem;
- channel growth precedes message/offer validation;
- affiliates or creators lack disclosure and claim controls.

## Tensions to test

- narrowly targeted lead magnet versus broad category reach;
- gated asset versus ungated reach/brand availability;
- high-effort qualification versus acquisition volume;
- immediate core-offer pitch versus staged education;
- content volume versus distinctive, memorable execution.
