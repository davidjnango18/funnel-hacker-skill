# Offers, Pricing, Bonuses, Guarantees, and Upsells

## Scope

Synthesis of Hormozi Offers (`SRC-008`), Dunford (`SRC-005`), MECLABS (`SRC-012`), Cialdini (`SRC-003`), and Brunson (`SRC-006`).

## Integrated offer model

1. **Comparison:** identify the real alternative, including inaction.
2. **Outcome:** state the desired result without overclaiming.
3. **Capability:** show what the product uniquely does relative to that alternative.
4. **Proof:** support outcome, mechanism, and fit.
5. **Time/effort:** disclose and reduce delay, setup, learning, and ongoing work.
6. **Risk allocation:** make terms, guarantee, cancellation, and responsibility clear.
7. **Price and payment:** connect price to the chosen frame without false anchors.
8. **Enhancements:** add only relevant bonuses, real urgency/scarcity, and coherent upsells.
9. **Fulfillment economics:** validate capacity, margin, support, refunds, activation, and retention.

## Value diagnostic

Use the Hormozi variables qualitatively:

- desired outcome;
- perceived likelihood;
- time delay;
- effort and sacrifice.

Then apply MECLABS:

- motivation;
- comparative value proposition;
- incentive;
- friction;
- anxiety.

Neither framework calculates conversion or willingness to pay.

## Bonus test

A bonus should remove a specific obstacle, accelerate time-to-value, reduce effort, add required proof/support, or serve a distinct use case. “More items” is not inherently more value.

## Guarantee test

Document:

- eligible customer and result/condition;
- exact remedy;
- time window and required steps;
- exclusions and burden of proof;
- operational cost and abuse risk;
- whether the promise is legally/commercially supportable.

A guarantee can reduce anxiety while adding procedural friction. Audit both.

## Upsell test

The next offer should extend the same job, solve the next legitimate problem, or improve delivery. Surprise, incompatibility, or obscured recurring terms are rejection signals.

## Cross-source tensions

- Dunford may support premium pricing through a favorable category frame; actual willingness to pay still needs research.
- Cialdini explains scarcity effects; scarcity must remain true.
- Brunson's ladder supports ascension; every extra offer also adds cognitive and operational complexity.
- Sharp warns that an incomparable premium offer still needs broad enough reach and availability to grow.
