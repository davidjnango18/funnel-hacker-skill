## Why this source matters

Sharp is a counterweight to funnel frameworks that optimize only immediate response or narrowly defined loyal segments. The book argues from recurring purchase patterns that growth usually requires more buyers, broad category reach, recognizable brand assets, and easy buying—not only deeper loyalty among existing customers.

## Core concepts

- **Double jeopardy:** smaller brands tend to have fewer buyers and slightly lower loyalty metrics; penetration is the dominant difference.
- **Acquisition and light buyers:** growth normally depends on reaching category buyers, including light and occasional buyers, not only heavy users.
- **Buyer overlap:** competing brands often sell to broadly similar customer bases; claimed psychographic uniqueness may be overstated.
- **Distinctiveness versus differentiation:** easy recognition can be more scalable than relying on every buyer to perceive meaningful uniqueness.
- **Mental availability:** the brand is noticed or retrieved in relevant buying situations.
- **Physical availability:** the brand is easy to find and buy across relevant situations and channels.
- **Continuous reach:** advertising can refresh memory structures even when immediate response is not visible.

## Frameworks

### Availability diagnostic

1. Define category entry/buying situations.
2. Check whether intended buyers can notice and retrieve the brand in those situations.
3. Audit consistent distinctive assets and unambiguous branding.
4. Check whether the offer can actually be bought with low search/access effort.
5. Measure reach and penetration alongside response and retention metrics.

### Growth interpretation guardrail

When a competitor shows strong loyalty, repeat buying, or engagement, ask whether those metrics are partly consequences of brand size and penetration. Do not automatically infer a unique loyalty mechanism.

## Diagnostic signals

- broad versus over-narrow audience reach;
- consistent colors, shapes, names, devices, sounds, or visual codes;
- clear brand attribution in performance creatives;
- presence across buying situations, channels, devices, and payment contexts;
- friction between ad availability and actual product availability;
- metrics limited to existing customers while category penetration is ignored;
- promotions that lift short-run sales without evidence of durable availability.

## Funnel and creative applications

- Preserve distinctive brand assets across ads, landers, checkout, email, and retargeting.
- Do not let conversion optimization erase recognition or category cues.
- Evaluate whether tight targeting excludes plausible category buyers and light buyers.
- Separate immediate response lift from memory/availability effects.
- For digital products, interpret physical availability broadly: device access, checkout reliability, local payments, delivery, support, and purchase-time availability.

## Questions this source helps answer

- Is the growth plan seeking more buyers or only more activity from current buyers?
- Can category buyers recognize and buy the brand easily?
- Are “loyalty” differences exceptional after accounting for brand size?
- Does the creative build the brand as well as communicate the offer?

## Misuse risks and limitations

- Do not turn empirical regularities into immutable laws for every market, time horizon, or business model.
- Much evidence is from established consumer categories; B2B, early-stage, luxury, marketplace, subscription, and infoproduct contexts can differ.
- Broad reach does not mean undifferentiated copy, universal channels, or wasteful buying.
- Distinctiveness does not eliminate the need for value, proof, product quality, or useful positioning.
- The supplied datasets are not current Mexico/Brazil benchmarks.

## Relationship with other sources

- **Dunford (`SRC-005`)** emphasizes choosing context and best-fit customers; Sharp emphasizes scalable reach and category penetration. They can coexist as positioning clarity plus sufficiently broad acquisition.
- **Hopkins (`SRC-001`)** centers measurable response; Sharp warns that immediate response is not the whole advertising effect.
- **Brunson/Hormozi (`SRC-006`, `SRC-008`, `SRC-009`)** optimize funnel economics and offers; Sharp asks whether that system also builds availability and reaches new buyers.

## Key operational takeaways

- Add penetration, reach, recognition, and availability to the funnel scorecard.
- Treat brand codes as conversion assets, not decoration.
- Avoid assuming that competitor engagement or repeat behavior proves a unique loyalty strategy.
