# Follow-Up and Communication Sequences

## Scope

Synthesis of Brunson (`SRC-006`), Walker (`SRC-007`), Cialdini (`SRC-003`), and CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`). Covers email/owned follow-up after permission. It does not define current deliverability or consent law.

## Sequence jobs

- orient and set expectations;
- deliver the promised asset or access;
- continue unresolved education/proof;
- answer observed objections;
- announce a real offer or event;
- support activation and fulfillment;
- recover a genuinely incomplete action;
- retain or reactivate based on consent and relevance.

## Diagnostic record

For every message capture timing, trigger, audience state, promise, content job, CTA, next state, and evidence. Distinguish broadcast cadence from behavior-triggered flow.

## Practitioner patterns

- Brunson's **Soap Opera Sequence** emphasizes serialized narrative and open loops early in the relationship.
- Brunson's **Daily Seinfeld Sequence** emphasizes ongoing personality-led observation tied to relevant offers.
- Walker's launch emails coordinate content releases, open cart, objections, and close.

These are templates, not default frequencies. Adapt to audience expectations, product risk, brand voice, consent, and measured engagement.

## Guardrails

- Never invent an event, inventory limit, deadline, testimonial, or reply history.
- Do not convert one opt-in into unrelated channel consent.
- Deliver the promised item before using obligation pressure.
- Do not infer intent from an open event alone; tracking can be incomplete or privacy-affected.
- Monitor downstream complaints, unsubscribes, deliverability, activation, refunds, and support—not clicks alone.
