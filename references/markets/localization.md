# Market Localization: Mexico, Brazil, and Cross-Market Use

## Scope and evidence gap

The ingested static corpus contains no dedicated Mexico or Brazil market report and no source by Érico Rocha, Pedro Sobral, or Vilma Núñez. Therefore this note defines a research and adaptation protocol, not cultural facts or market benchmarks. Use [live-sources.md](../live-sources.md) for current evidence and the orchestrator's `es-MX` or `pt-BR` market profile for task constraints.

## Separation rule

Keep these artifacts distinct:

1. original-language evidence;
2. literal/semantic translation;
3. extracted persuasion or funnel principle;
4. market evidence for the target locale;
5. localization hypothesis;
6. original target-market execution.

## Market research checklist

- current competitor ads and destinations in the target country;
- local search, creator, commerce, and channel behavior;
- currency, taxes, installments, payment methods, checkout availability, and refunds;
- logistics/delivery and support expectations;
- current platform rules and advertising restrictions;
- claims/testimonial/consumer-protection requirements;
- local language, register, objection, and proof preferences derived from evidence;
- niche-specific seasonality and buying events.

## Non-generalization rule

Do not generalize US → Mexico, US → Brazil, Brazil → Mexico, or Mexico → Brazil. A framework may transfer as a hypothesis; claims, examples, economics, payment context, cadence, and execution require local verification.

## Static versus live

- Awareness, positioning, persuasion, value, and experimentation frameworks can guide questions.
- Active ads, platform features, trends, benchmarks, payment options, and current behavior require fresh sources.
- If a relevant local source is absent, mark the gap; do not fill it with stereotypes.

## Recommended specialists

`translation` plus one market profile and one asset-specific specialist. Add collection/research skills only as required by the question.
