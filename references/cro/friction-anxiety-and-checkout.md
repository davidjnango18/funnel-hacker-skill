# Friction, Anxiety, and Checkout Decisions

## Scope

Primary lens: MECLABS (`SRC-012`). Research method: CXL (`SRC-010`, `SRC-011`). Supporting ethics/proof: Cialdini (`SRC-003`) and Hormozi Offers (`SRC-008`).

## Distinguish the barriers

- **Friction:** time, effort, fields, steps, ambiguity, technical difficulty, payment effort, or cognitive load.
- **Anxiety:** fear or uncertainty about security, legitimacy, fit, consequences, hidden terms, performance, privacy, or regret.
- **Low motivation:** insufficient desire or urgency for the outcome.
- **Weak value:** the exchange is unclear or inferior to alternatives.

Reducing one category does not automatically repair another.

## Checkout audit

1. Is product, price, billing period, currency, tax/shipping, renewal, and cancellation visible before commitment?
2. Does the page preserve the ad/offer promise and brand identity?
3. Are payment methods and delivery available for the target market now?
4. Are required fields necessary, understandable, and error-tolerant?
5. Are security/privacy statements accurate and placed near the concern?
6. Are guarantee terms clear and usable?
7. Are order bumps/upsells optional, compatible, and non-deceptive?
8. Is the confirmation/fulfillment step verified?
9. Are abandonment, payment failure, fraud, refunds, support, and downstream value measured?

## Research signals

- field-level errors and device/browser failures;
- abandonment by payment method/market;
- user-test confusion and support questions;
- on-site survey objections;
- replay evidence of repeated attempts or loops;
- mismatch between displayed and charged terms;
- trust/proof interaction close to the decision.

## Common false solutions

- removing useful detail merely to shorten the page;
- adding generic badges without relevance;
- using a discount to compensate for unclear value;
- hiding costs until the final step;
- adding a guarantee that is hard to claim;
- copying a competitor checkout without knowing its constraints or outcomes.
